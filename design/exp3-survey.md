# C&C: Ancients Expansion 3 (The Roman Civil Wars): scenario survey

Compiled 2026-10-10 from commandsandcolors.net. Sources: the 19 standard scenario pages (301–319) and the 4 Epic pages (EPIC01–04), their map images (`CCA_maps/<code>.jpg`), the Expansion 3 box pages (Contents, Epic Ancients, Official Scenarios, New Special Rules, New Terrain Hexes), the Expansion 2 box pages that Expansion 3 depends on (Contents, New Special Rules, New Terrain Hex, New Unit Type, Special Block Mechanic), the site's terrain pages, GMT's Expansion 2 & 3 Combo product page and the GMT living rules (2009). Everything outside the scenario pages is marked where it is used. All rule text below is paraphrased.

## How to read this

- **Blocks** come from the page CSS class of each War Council army box, checked against the unit-table icon file names and the map colours:
  - `ban0_rom` = **grey Roman** (core-game "Republican" Roman blocks; icons `rom_*`, block icon `BL_rom.jpg`).
  - `ban2_rom` = **red Roman** (Expansion 2 "Marian" Roman blocks; icons `rom2_*`, block icon `BL_rom2.jpg`).
  - `ban2_bar` = **Barbarian green** (Expansion 2; icons `bar_*`).
  - `ban1_eas` = Eastern Kingdom tan, `ban0_car` = Carthaginian brown, `ban1_grk` = Greek blue (Epic pages only, plus 316).
  - Colour check: the dominant pixel colour of `BL_rom.jpg` is grey and of `BL_rom2.jpg` is red, and the EPIC03 War Council calls `BL_rom2` "Roman (red) blocks". GMT's product page confirms the pairing: grey Republican Romans from the core game, red Marian Romans from Expansion 2, and Expansion 3 adds more of both (Contents: 60 red + 40 grey small blocks, 9 + 9 medium, 2 large red).
  - In every Roman-vs-Roman battle the **top army is grey (`ban0_rom`) and the bottom army is red (`ban2_rom`)**.
- **Top/bottom** comes from the map banner. A star in the top-left or bottom-left corner marks the side that moves first; it agreed with the War Council on all 23 pages. On every page with a unit table, the first row of the table is the top army.
- **Unit counts** are the numbers in the page's unit table (icon + count). Every map (301–319) was counted and cross-checked against its table. Mismatches are listed under each scenario and in section 4. The Epic pages have no unit table, so their counts were taken from the map.
- **Codes:** LI, LB (light bow), LS (light sling), AX, WA, MI, HI, LC, MC, HC, EL, HCH, HWM, LDR. **No new unit codes are needed**: Expansion 3 uses only existing unit types. What is new is the abilities that the Marius / Julian rules give Roman MI and HI (section 3.1). The Expansion 2 Barbarian Light Chariot does not appear in any Expansion 3 battle.
- "Not stated" means the page does not say.
- **Book vs web:** the Contents page lists "1 Rule and Scenario Book containing 19 battle scenarios", "1 Epic Rule Book" and "2 Double sided Epic Scenario sheets containing 4 battle scenarios". Pages 301–319 were published 11–13 May 2008 and EPIC01–04 on 23–24 October 2008. All 23 carry the "Expansion 3" tag and are written by "GMT Games". The tag search for Expansion 3 returns exactly these 23, and the site's full scenario list has no 3xx number above 319, so **there are no later web or bonus additions**. (EPIC05 and later exist on the site but belong to later boxes and are not tagged Expansion 3.) GMT's pre-release blurb promised "at least 16 scenarios" starting at Aesis River (82 BC); no Aesis River scenario exists in the final list.
- **Prerequisites (Expansion 3 box page):** the standard battles need the core game plus Expansion 2 (red Roman and green Barbarian blocks, marsh, the Marius and Caesar rules). One battle (316 Zela) uses Expansion 1 blocks, though other blocks may be substituted. The Epic battles also need Expansions 1 and 2.
- **Command cards:** the Contents list no new cards, so the 60 base cards are enough. Hand sizes are 4, 5 or 6, with two exceptions: 313 (Caesarian hand grows from 2 to 5) and 310 (the Numidians have no cards and order by dice). Epic hands are 6–9.

### 301 Colline Gate (82 BC)
Page: `/ancients/maps/second-mithridatic-war-83-82-bc/301-colline-gate-82-bc.html` · Map: `/ancients/images/stories/CCA_maps/301.jpg`
- **Top:** Samnite Army, **grey Roman blocks** (`ban0_rom`). Leader (page): Pontius. Map leaders: Pontius. **5 cards. Moves first.**
- **Bottom:** Roman Army (Sulla), **red Roman blocks** (`ban2_rom`). Leader (page): Sulla. Map leaders: Sulla, Crassus. **6 cards.**
- **Victory:** 5 banners.
- **Special rules:**
  - The walls of Rome (drawn with rampart hexes) are impassable.
  - **Marius Legions** for both armies.
  - **Camp capture (Roman only):** if a Roman unit is on an enemy camp hex **at the start of the Roman turn**, the Roman gains 1 permanent banner; the camp tile is removed and the banner collected before a card is played.
- **Units:**
  - Samnite: LS 1, AX 6, MI 2, MC 2, LDR 1.
  - Roman: LS 2, AX 1, MI 6, MC 2, LDR 2.
  - Map matches the table.
- **Terrain:**
  - Wall of Rome: about 10 rampart hexes along the Roman row 8 (two angled corner pieces flank a 2-hex gap in the centre, where a Roman MI and AX stand). Sulla's MC is behind the wall.
  - 2 Samnite camps (tents) in the top row.
  - Otherwise plain.
- **New needs:**
  - Grey vs red Roman blocks (Samnites in grey).
  - **Marius Legions** (MI pilum).
  - **Impassable wall hexes** (rampart art, impassable; LOS not stated).
  - **Start-of-turn camp capture with tile removal**, one side only.

### 306 Bibracte (58 BC)
Page: `/ancients/maps/gallic-wars-59-51-bc/306-bibracte-58-bc.html` · Map: `/ancients/images/stories/CCA_maps/306.jpg`
- **Top:** Helvetii, **Barbarian green blocks** (`ban2_bar`). Leader (page): "Various Tribal Chiefs". Map leaders: two unnamed (each on an MC). **4 cards. Moves first.**
- **Bottom:** Roman Army, red Roman blocks. Leader (page): Julius Caesar. Map leaders: Caesar, two unnamed. **6 cards.**
- **Victory:** 7 banners.
- **Special rules:**
  - **Julian Legions** and **Julius Caesar** rules in effect.
  - **Immobile recruits:** the two Roman AX in the Roman camp hexes may not move or advance out of their hexes, but they may fire, close combat and battle back.

- **Units:**
  - Helvetii: LI 2, AX 7, WA 9, MC 3, LDR 2.
  - Roman: LI 2, LB 2, AX 4, MI 4, HI 2, MC 2, **HWM 1 (red Roman)**, LDR 3.
  - Map matches the table.

- **Terrain:**
  - About 21 hills forming a ridge across the Roman half (rows 7–9). Most Roman units start on them.
  - 5 forest hexes: 2 on the left edge, 3 on the right.
  - Camps: 2 Helvetii camps in the top row (one empty with tents, one holding a WA), and 2 Roman camps on the bottom row (each holding a recruit AX).
- **New needs:**
  - **Barbarian green blocks.**
  - Julian Legions.
  - Caesar.
  - **Units that may not move** (but may fight).
  - Start-of-turn camp capture with tile removal.
  - Red Roman HWM art.

### 308 Gergovia (52 BC)
Page: `/ancients/maps/gallic-wars-59-51-bc/308-gergovia-52-bc.html` · Map: `/ancients/images/stories/CCA_maps/308.jpg`
- **Top:** Gallic Army, Barbarian green blocks. Leader (page): Vercingetorix. Map leaders: Vercingetorix (off-board, with the reinforcements), Critognatus (map: "Critignatus"), Vercassivellaunus. **5 cards.**
- **Bottom:** Roman Army, red Roman blocks. Leader (page): Julius Caesar. Map leaders: Caesar, Sextus, Manucius. **5 cards. Moves first.**
- **Victory:** 8 banners.
- **Special rules:**
  - **Gallic reinforcements:**
    - The 4 off-board Gallic units plus Vercingetorix enter when the Gaul plays **any Leadership card**.
    - They are set on any of 5 marked hexes on the Gallic right (map labels G1–G5), with Vercingetorix attached to one of them.
    - They may not move further that turn, but may battle.
  - **Roman reinforcement:** before the Roman's next turn after that, the Roman must place his one off-board unit on any baseline hex in his left section. It then joins the army.
  - Julius Caesar and Julian Legions rules.
  - **Gallic camps (Roman objective):** there are 3 Gallic camp hexes.
    - A Roman unit on one at the start of the Roman turn removes the camp tile.
    - The **first** camp removed scores nothing; the **second and third** each score 1 permanent banner.
  - **City wall hexes (Roman objective):** a Roman unit on a city wall hex scores 1 banner, which is **lost if the unit moves off or is eliminated**.
  - **Roman camps (Gallic objectives):**
    - A Gallic unit on an enemy camp hex at the start of a turn removes the tile and scores 1 permanent banner.
    - A Gallic unit on a **Roman large-camp rampart hex** scores 1 banner, lost if the unit moves off or is eliminated.
  - The Gallic camp hexes and the Gergovia wall hexes count as **hill** hexes as well.
  - **Scalable city walls** rule in effect for Gergovia.
  - **Steep slopes:**
    - A hill hex adjacent to a forest hex is impassable to units moving from that forest hex.
    - Units on such a hill may move or retreat down into the forest.
    - No close combat is possible between such a hill hex and an adjacent forest hex, in either direction.
- **Units:**
  - Gallic: LS 1, LB 2, AX 7, WA 8, MC 2, LDR 3.
    - This includes the **off-board reinforcements: AX 2, WA 2 + Vercingetorix**, drawn above the top edge.
  - Roman: LI 2, LB 2, MI 8, **WA 2 (red Roman)**, HI 2, MC 2, **HWM 2 (red Roman)**, LDR 3.
    - This includes the **off-board reinforcement: MI 1**, drawn below the bottom-left edge.
  - Map matches the table when the off-board units are counted.
  - Both Roman HI are labelled "Legio X" (one inside a camp hex, one with Caesar).
- **Terrain:**
  - **Gergovia city walls:** 5 rampart-tile hexes in the top centre (2 corners and 3 straight), 2 of them holding Gallic LB.
  - A hill mass of about 25 hexes below the walls (top centre).
  - **3 Gallic camps** (palisade outlines) on the hills, each holding a WA.
  - About 10 forest hexes flanking the hill mass on both sides (about 5 top-left, about 5 on the right).
  - 5 broken-ground hexes across the centre (row 6).
  - Entry hexes G1–G5 on the left edge: G1 and G5 are plain, G2–G4 are hills.
  - **1 Roman camp hex** (the HI "Legio X" stands in it).
  - **Roman large camp:** 3 rampart hexes on a diagonal in the bottom-right corner, holding HWM, LI and HWM, facing up-left.
- **New needs:**
  - Barbarian blocks.
  - Julian Legions and Caesar.
  - **Scalable city walls** (new terrain).
  - **Terrain stacking:** camp + hill, and wall + hill.
  - **Directional steep slopes** between hill and forest.
  - **Reinforcements:** an off-board group entering on any Leadership card at fixed hexes, followed by a forced Roman placement.
  - **Camp-count banners:** the 1st camp scores none, the 2nd and 3rd score 1 each.
  - **Temporary occupation banners** (wall hex, large-camp rampart hex).
  - Start-of-turn camp capture with tile removal.
  - Red Roman WA and HWM art.

### 309 Ilerda (49 BC)

- **Special rules:**
  - The city walls of Ilerda (rampart hexes) are **scalable**.
  - The river is impassable.

  - **Camps:** a unit on an enemy camp hex at the start of its turn removes the tile. When **both** enemy camps are removed, the side scores 1 banner.

- **Units:**
  - Pompeian: LB 2, AX 2, MI 5, HI 4, MC 2, LDR 3.
  - Caesarian: LS 1, AX 2, MI 4, HI 6, MC 2, LDR 3.

  - Impassable river down the right edge (about 9 hexes).

  - 2 Pompeian camps in the top row (each holding an MI) and 2 Caesarian camps (tents) in the bottom row.
- **New needs:**
  - Scalable city walls.
  - Julian Legions, Caesar.
  - **"Remove all enemy camps → 1 banner".**
  - **Area-majority banner** (hillock, one side only).
  - Start-of-turn camp capture with tile removal.

### 310 Utica P1 Delaying Action (49 BC)
Page: `/ancients/maps/caesars-civil-war-49-45-bc/310-utica-p1-delaying-action-49-bc.html` · Map: `/ancients/images/stories/CCA_maps/310.jpg`
- **Top:** Numidian Army, grey Roman blocks. Leader (page): Saburra (the map spells it "Suburra"). **No Command cards: rolls 2 dice to order units.**
- **Bottom:** Caesarian Army, red Roman blocks. Leader (page): "Curio's cavalry lieutenant" (unnamed on the map). **6 cards. Moves first.**
- **Victory:** 4 banners.
- **Special rules:**
  - The scenario is meant for **solo play**. Only the Roman side holds cards.
  - **Dice orders:** at the start of each Numidian turn, roll 2 dice. Each die does one thing:
    - green circle: one light unit moves and battles;
    - blue triangle: one medium unit moves and battles;
    - red square: one heavy unit moves and battles;
    - helmet: the leader is ordered, together with his unit if attached;
    - flag: one chosen unit must retreat a full move (any unit may be chosen, even one that cannot retreat or stands on the edge);
    - crossed swords: one chosen unit loses 1 block.
    - The (solo) player picks which unit of each type is affected.
  - The river is impassable.
  - **No Marius/Julian rule is listed** (the tags omit Julian Legion; the Caesarian side has no infantry).
- **Units:**
  - Numidian: LB 1, AX 2, MI 3, HI 1, LC 3, MC 3, LDR 1.
  - Caesarian: LC 2, MC 4, LDR 1.
  - Map matches the table.
- **Terrain:**
  - An impassable river winding from the top centre-right to the right edge (about 9 hexes).
  - 3 hills on the left.
- **New needs:**
  - **A side that orders by a 2-dice roll instead of cards**, with forced-retreat and block-loss faces.
  - A "who chooses" rule for 2-player or AI use.

### 313 Brindisi Raid (48 BC)
Page: `/ancients/maps/caesars-civil-war-49-45-bc/313-brindisi-raid-48-bc.html` · Map: `/ancients/images/stories/CCA_maps/313.jpg`
- **Top:** Pompeian Army, grey Roman blocks. Leader: Libo. **5 cards. Moves first.**
- **Bottom:** Caesarian Army, red Roman blocks. Leader (page): Antony. Map leaders: Antony, Fufius. **"2* (5)" cards.**
- **Victory:** 6 banners.
- **Special rules:**
  - **Hand growth:** the Caesarian player starts with 2 cards. Each turn he plays 1 and draws 2, until he holds 5.
  - Julian Legions for both armies.
  - **Brindisi ramparts:** the units on them at the start are Caesarian, although Brindisi lies on the Pompeian side, and they retreat toward the Caesarian edge.
  - **Ship markers (both sides' objectives):**
    - A spare block sits on each seacoast hex **inside** the Brindisi ramparts (Caesarian transports). A Pompeian unit on one at the start of the Pompeian turn scores 1 permanent banner, and the marker is removed.
    - A spare block sits on each seacoast hex **outside** Brindisi (Pompeian marine landing hexes). A Caesarian unit on one at the start of the Caesarian turn scores 1 permanent banner, and the marker is removed.
  - **All seacoast hexes count as fordable river** for movement and combat.
- **Units:**
  - Pompeian: LB 1, AX 4, MI 6, LDR 1.
  - Caesarian: LI 3, AX 1, MI 8, MC 2, LDR 2.
  - Map matches the table. The ship blocks (4 grey, 4 red) are markers, not units.
- **Terrain:**
  - **Seacoast:** 4 hexes in the top-left corner (grey markers) and 4 hexes in the top-right inside Brindisi (red markers).
  - **Brindisi ramparts:** about 8 rampart hexes running diagonally down from the top row (left of centre), then along row 3 to the right edge. Caesarian LI 3 and AX 1 stand on 4 of them.
  - A full row of 12 hills across the board (row 6).
  - **4 camp hexes** (tents) in the Caesarian row 7. **Antony and Fufius each stand alone** in one; Caesarian MI hold the other two. The page gives no camp rule.
- **New needs:**

## 1b. Epic scenarios (separate game mode)

These four use the **Commands & Colors: Epic Ancients** rules (Expansion 3's Epic Rule Book; summarised on the "EPIC ANCIENTS" box page):
- The board is 26 hexes wide and 9 deep: the Expansion 2 and Expansion 3 boards joined, with left, centre and right sections.
- Up to 3 **Field Command** cards may be played per turn, one per section. Alternatively, one **Army Command** card may be played; it orders as many units as the number of cards in hand.

## 3. Consolidated engine gaps

Everything below is **NEW** unless marked "existing".

### 3.1 New unit types and unit abilities

**No new unit types.** All units are LI, LS, LB, AX, WA, MI, HI, LC, MC, HC, EL, HCH, HWM or LDR. What is new is the abilities that rules give Roman legions:

**Marius Legions rule** (Expansion 2/3 special-rules pages; used in 301–305, "for both armies"):

| Property | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who | Medium infantry (MI). The rule names "Medium Roman Infantry", but these pages apply it to both armies (including the Samnite MI in 301). |
| Ranged combat | Becomes a missile unit (pilum). **Range 2**, **1 die** whether it held or moved its 1 hex. Unlike other missile units, it never gets 2 dice for standing still. |
| Movement and close combat | Unchanged (1 hex, 4 dice). |

**Julian Legions rule** (Expansion 3 special-rules page; used in 306–309 and 311–319, but not in 310):

| Property | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who | Roman **HI and MI**. Scope per page: "in effect" (306–308), "for both armies / both camps" (309, 311–315, 318, 319), "for the Romans" (316, 317). |
| Ranged combat | As Marius: range 2, 1 die if it held or moved 1 hex; **no fire after a 2-hex move**. |
| Movement | Either move 1 hex and close combat as normal, **or move 2 hexes and not battle**. |
| Retreat, dice, class | Unchanged (HI 5 dice, MI 4; heavy/medium for hits and cards). |

- The page notes that the Julian rule can be retrofitted to Expansion 2 battles from River Sabis on, except the Teutoburg pair. This does not matter for Expansion 3.
- **Tenth Legion (Expansion 2 special unit):** HI with pilum, 5 dice, may move 2 without battling, retreats 1. The maps for 306, 307 and 308 label both Roman HI "Legio X", but **no page gives a Tenth Legion special rule**. Under the Julian rule every Roman HI already has exactly these abilities, so the label is cosmetic.
- Engine side effects:
  - MI/HI become missile units for Darken the Sky, presumably (not stated).
  - They are not light, so Order Light Troops and Move-Fire-Move do not apply.
  - The existing `immortals` elite is the closest precedent (an MI that fires), but the range (2 vs 3) and the dice (always 1) differ, and Julian adds the 2-hex move. A per-scenario rule flag that applies to all MI/HI of the named side(s) fits better than per-unit elite markers.

**Unit art needed:**
- Grey Roman: LI, LS, LB, AX, MI, **WA**, HI, LC, MC, **EL**, **HWM**, leader.
- Red Roman: LI, LS, LB, AX, MI, **WA**, HI, LC, MC, **HWM**, leader, plus a **Caesar portrait leader** (the site uses a special `rom2_caesar.jpg` block).
- Barbarian green: LI, LS, LB, AX, WA, MC, leader.
- Pontic (Eastern tan): LI, AX, MI, HI, LC, HC, HCH, leader.

### 3.2 New terrain

- **Scalable city walls** (308 Gergovia, 309 Ilerda). Drawn with rampart tiles. Rules from the Expansion 3 New Terrain page:
  - **Movement (foot units except war machines, and leaders):**
    - May enter only if the move starts adjacent to the wall hex, and must stop on entering.
    - A unit leaving the wall hex may move only 1 hex and stops.
    - A retreating or evading foot unit does not stop.
  - **Mounted units and war machines:**
    - The wall hex is impassable to them, and they may not retreat or evade onto it.
    - They may close combat a unit on it only if they began the turn adjacent.
  - **Close combat:**
    - Maximum 2 dice for either side (attacking a unit on the wall, or a unit on the wall attacking out).
    - The defender on the wall ignores 1 sword and may ignore 1 flag from any adjacent attacker.
    - **The defender strikes first**, as if it held First Strike, unless the attacker is itself on an adjacent wall hex.
  - **Ranged:** the defender may ignore 1 flag. Adjacent enemies prevent firing as usual.
  - Card or leader bonus dice raise the cap.
  - **Line of sight:** blocks LOS through it, but not along the same wall.
- **Fortified city walls / impassable towns** (301 walls of Rome, 303 Lauron, 304 Valentia). The pages say only "impassable".
  - The site's Fortified City Walls page says such walls are impassable to all units, allow no battle, and **block LOS**.
  - `steep` (impassable, blocks LOS) is the closest existing terrain. The art is rampart or town pieces.
  - Whether 301–304 should block LOS is not stated on those pages.
- **Terrain stacking** (308):
  - The 3 Gallic camp hexes are also hills.
  - The Gergovia wall hexes are also hills (scalable wall + hill).
  - The engine has one terrain per hex today. The combined dice and protection effects are not defined.
- **Hill–forest steep edges** (308):
  - A hexside between a hill and a forest is impassable going up (forest → hill) but passable going down (hill → forest, by move or retreat).
  - No close combat is allowed across it in either direction.
  - This needs per-hexside data, like the existing rampart edges.
- **Seacoast treated as fordable river** (313): movement and combat follow the fordable-river rules (stop on entry, close-combat cap 2, ranged cap 1). Existing `river` with `ford` could carry sea art.
- **Removable camp tiles** (301, 306, 308, 309, 316): capturing a camp removes the tile, so the hex becomes plain mid-game.
- **Existing:**
  - rampart: 308 large camp (3 hexes), 313 Brindisi (about 8), 314 Caesar's line (10);
  - marsh: 315 (1), 318 (about 9);
  - sea: 313, 318;
  - steep / impassable hills: 315;
  - fordable river: 302;
  - impassable river: 309, 310;
  - camps, hills, forest, broken ground (308).

### 3.3 New special-rule mechanisms

1. **Julius Caesar leader trait** (306–309, 314–319):
   - The unit he is attached to rolls **+1 close-combat die**, even when terrain caps the dice. This is the same as the existing Alexander `ccBonus`, added after the caps.
   - **New:** a foot unit he is attached to (not a war machine) may **move 2 hexes and still close combat**. This matters for MI, HI (Julian or not) and AX. WA can already move 2 and battle.
2. **Marius / Julian legion rules** (3.1). These are per-scenario flags that name which side's MI/HI they cover (both, or Romans only).
3. **Start-of-turn occupation scoring** (several variants). The existing camp capture scores at the **end of the move**; these score at the **start of the occupier's turn**, before a card is played:
   - Camp capture with **tile removal** and a permanent banner: 301 (Roman only), 306 (both sides), 316 (both sides), 308 (Gauls on the Roman camp).
   - **Camp count:** 308 Romans — the 1st Gallic camp scores nothing, the 2nd and 3rd score 1 each. 309 — 1 banner once **both** enemy camps are removed.
   - **Ship markers** (313): 4 marker hexes per side, permanent banner, marker removed.
4. **Temporary occupation banners** (the banner is lost when the unit leaves or is eliminated):
   - 308: a Roman unit on a city wall hex; a Gallic unit on a Roman large-camp rampart hex.
   - 311: a Caesarian unit on the Pompeian camp.
5. **Area-majority banner** (309): Caesar's side scores 1 banner while it has more units on the 3-hex hillock than the enemy. Timing and losability are not stated.
6. **Exit-for-banner variants**. The existing 010 Castulo exit is the closest:
   - 311: Pompeian **cavalry only**, through 2 marked hexes at the left edge.
   - 314: Pompeian units, off "the Roman left section".
   - 317: Roman **units and leaders**, off the Numidian edge. A leader exit scores a banner.
   - The exiting piece is removed from play in all three.
7. **Reinforcements** (308). The existing 005 Trebbia ambush is the closest:
   - An off-board Gallic group (4 units + leader) enters on **any Leadership card**, onto 5 fixed hexes, with the leader attached to one unit. It may battle but not move further that turn.
   - Afterwards the Roman **must** place 1 off-board MI on a baseline hex of his left section before his next turn.
8. **Separate commands** (304): each Sertorian unit belongs to Herennius' or Perpenna's command, and a Command card may order units of only one command. It needs data, UI and AI support.
9. **Dice ordering instead of cards** (310):
   - The Numidian side holds no cards and each turn rolls 2 dice.
   - Each class symbol orders one unit of that class; a helmet orders the leader and his unit.
   - A flag forces a chosen unit to retreat a full move; crossed swords remove 1 block from a chosen unit.
   - The page frames this as solo play, where the human chooses for both sides. For an AI or 2-player game, who makes the choices must be defined.
10. **Hand growth** (313): 2 → 5, play 1 and draw 2 per turn. This generalises the existing 006 rule (2 → 4).
11. **Immobile units** (306): two AX may not move or advance, but may fire, close combat and battle back. Retreat is not addressed.
12. **Lone leaders at setup** (313: Antony and Fufius each alone in a camp). Expansion 1's 102 needed the same check.
13. **Units set up in the enemy half** (303 ambush force, 313 Brindisi garrison). The pages only confirm that they retreat toward their own edge, which is presumably the existing behaviour.
14. **Side naming:** in Roman-vs-Roman battles, rules that say "Roman" mean one specific side (301: Sulla's red army). Scenario data must name sides explicitly, not by faction.
15. **Not needed:**
    - No new command cards.
    - No new unit types.
    - No leader-loss card penalties.
    - No sudden-death leader rules.
    - No elephant fright rules.
    - The Expansion 2 Barbarian Light Chariot, Rolling Fire and "I Am Spartacus" Slave bonus do not appear in Expansion 3 battles.

### 3.4 New block colours and armies

- **Grey Roman** (`ban0_rom`, core game): Samnite (301), Sertorian (302–305), Numidian (310, 312, 317), Pompeian (309, 311, 313–315, 318, 319). That is the top army in 15 of 19 battles. It also appears in the Epic Roman armies.
- **Red Roman** (`ban2_rom`, Expansion 2/3): the bottom army in all 19 battles (Sulla 301, the Republic under Fufius and Pompey 302–305, Caesar 306–319).
- **Barbarian green** (`ban2_bar`, Expansion 2): Helvetii 306, Germanic 307, Gauls 308. **New block set.**
- **Eastern tan** (`ban1_eas`): Pontic 316. This is the existing `eas` set with a new Pontic look.
- **Engine conflict:** the engine's `rom` set is drawn **red** (`BLOCK_COLORS.rom` edge `#b81d24` in `src/art/palettes.ts`), although on the site and in the core game the base-game Romans are grey. Expansion 3 needs **two visually distinct Roman sets**. The natural mapping is:
  - keep `rom` (red) for the `ban2_rom` red side, which matches the engine's existing Roman look;
  - add a **new grey set** (for example `rmg`) for the `ban0_rom` side.
  The alternative is to recolour `rom` to grey and add a red set, but that would change the art of the base Roman armies, which `tests/art/looks.test.ts` pins.
- **304:** the map draws Perpenna's command in light-blue blocks to tell the two Sertorian commands apart. The engine needs some per-command marker (colour tint, flag or label).
- **Army looks:** Samnite, Sertorian (with Spanish WA), Numidian (including Juba's HI and elephants in grey blocks), Pompeian and Caesarian legions, Helvetii, Germanic, Gallic, Pontic. The two Roman armies could share the "roman" figure kit and differ only by block colour.

### 3.5 Epic Ancients (EPIC01–04)

This would be an entirely separate mode:
- a 26×9 board;
- multiple Field Command cards per turn (one per section), with special counting for Leadership, Line Command, Double Time, Coordinated Attack, Out Flanked and Inspired Field General plays;
- Army Command cards ordering "equal to command" (the cards in hand);
- Field General's Initiative die rolls;
- drawing 2 cards per turn up to the starting hand;
- First Strike at +2 dice;
- 12–14 banner targets, and mixed block sets per side.

There are no unit tables; the counts in 1b are map readings. **Recommend leaving Epic out of scope** for an Expansion 3 port.

## 4. Ambiguities, conflicts and things not determined

1. **314 Dyrrhachium:** the table has Pompeian LI 1 / LB 2, but the map shows **LI 2 / LB 1** (one light unit near Pompey is drawn "L", not "LB"). Which one is right was not determined.
2. **315 Pharsalus:** the table has **4 Caesarian leaders**, but the map shows 3 (Caesar, Antony, Calvinus).
3. **304 Valentia:** Perpenna's command is drawn in light-blue (Greek-style) blocks on the map, while the CSS and table icons are grey. This is read as a map convention for the second command, not a block set.
4. **Julian/Marius scope for non-Roman troops in Roman blocks:** the rules say "Roman" MI/HI, but the pages say "for both armies" where one army is Samnite (301), Numidian under Saburra (312, which has MI 1 and HI 1) or includes Juba's Numidian HI (318). Whether those MI/HI get the pilum and 2-hex move is not determined. 316 and 317 explicitly limit Julian to the Romans; 310 lists no legion rule.
5. **Pilum dice:** the text gives 1 die "when it holds or moves only one hex", so 1 die even when standing still. Whether pilum units count as missile units for Darken the Sky is not stated.
6. **Caesar:**
   - "+1 die including into/out of terrain that reduces dice" matches the engine's Alexander treatment (after caps).
   - Whether it applies to battle back, bonus combat or First Strike is not stated, as for Alexander.
   - The "move 2 and close combat" bonus needs him attached for the whole move; whether he may join mid-move is not stated.
7. **Start-of-turn capture:**
   - The camp pages say "occupies … at the start of the player's turn". The existing engine scores camp capture when a unit **ends its move** there (011, 114). Both versions are needed.
   - After tile removal the hex is presumably plain.
8. **308 Gergovia:**
   - Which hexes are "the" Roman camp and the "Roman large camp rampart hexes" is inferred from the map: 1 camp-outline hex holding Legio X, and 3 rampart hexes in the bottom-right corner.
   - Hill + camp and hill + wall combined effects are not defined.
   - The reinforcement entry on "any leadership card" does not say whether the card's other orders are also used, or whether an Inspired Left/Center/Right card must match the entry section (the G hexes are on the Gallic right).
   - The Roman placement does not say what happens if every left-section baseline hex is occupied.
   - Whether the Gallic camp-capture banner applies once or per Roman camp hex is not stated (the map shows one Roman camp hex).
9. **309 Ilerda:**
   - The hillock banner is not said to be permanent or losable, and its timing (start of turn? continuous?) is not stated.
   - "Caesar units" is read as the Caesarian side.
10. **310 Utica P1:**
    - The dice-order rules are written for solo play. In 2-player or AI play, who picks the unit for a flag or swords result is not stated.
    - "Retreat a full move" for a unit that cannot retreat presumably costs blocks per normal rules. Not stated.
11. **311 Utica P2:** "cavalry" presumably means LC and MC. The exit hexes are read from the map labels (left edge, rows 5–6). P1 and P2 are not linked on the pages.
12. **313 Brindisi:**
    - Seacoast "as fordable river" for movement and combat; LOS is not mentioned.
    - The 4 camp hexes on the Caesarian line have no rule.
    - The rampart facing must be read from the art. The garrison is inside the town, so the walls presumably protect the hexsides toward the Pompeian approach: west for the diagonal pieces, south for the row-3 pieces. Not confirmed.
13. **314 Dyrrhachium:** "exits off the Roman left section" is read as the Caesarian baseline hexes in the Caesarian left section (bottom-left of the map). The side edge is not mentioned.
14. **317 Ruspina:** leaders exiting count as banners. Whether a unit with an attached leader scores 2 is not stated.
15. **306 Bibracte:** whether the immobile recruits may retreat (retreat is not movement), and what happens if they cannot, is not stated.
16. **301, 303, 304:** impassable wall and town hexes; LOS blocking not stated on these pages.
17. **315:** impassable hills (as `steep`, which blocks LOS); the scenario page does not mention LOS.
18. **Names and spellings:**
    - 310 "Suburra" on the map vs "Saburra" on the page.
    - 304's rule text "Herrenius" and "Pepperna".
    - 308 map "Critignatus".
    - 314 "Titus Pulieo".
    - 316 "Drmchtes".
    - 313 "Fufius".
    - EPIC01 "Varrus" on the page vs Varro on the map, and Paullus drawn as "Paillius".
    - The map banners on 309–311 read "CESARIAN".
    - 311's page tags include "Macedonian" in error.
    - 312 "Bagradas River" has no river on its map.
    - 301's Marius rule link points to the Expansion 4 special-rules page; the same text is on the Expansion 2 and 3 pages.
19. **Legio X labels** (306, 307, 308): two HI per map carry the label, and no special rule is given. They are read as cosmetic (Julian already gives the Tenth's abilities).
20. **Epic counts** are map readings only (no tables), at 1920×652.
21. Terrain counts written "about N" are approximate readings of the map images. Exact hex coordinates were not extracted.
