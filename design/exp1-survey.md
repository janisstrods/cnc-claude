# C&C: Ancients Expansion 1 (Greece & Eastern Kingdoms): scenario survey

Compiled 2026-10-09 from commandsandcolors.net. Sources: the 24 scenario pages, their map images (`CCA_maps/<code>.jpg`), the Expansion 1 box pages (Contents, New Game Mechanic, New Unit Types, Official Scenarios list), and the site's unit and terrain pages. One extra source was used for war-machine rules: a third-party transcription of the core rulebook (rulespal.com). Everything outside the scenario pages is marked where it is used.

## How to read this

- **Blocks** come from the War Council text ("Use … blocks") where the page gives it. The page CSS class of each army box also encodes it: `ban1_grk` = Greek (light blue), `ban1_eas` = Eastern Kingdom (light tan), `ban0_car` = Carthaginian, `ban0_rom` = Roman (grey). Map block colours were checked against this.
- **Top/bottom** comes from the map. The army banner sits at the top or bottom edge, and a star in the top-left or bottom-left corner marks the side that moves first. On every page that has a unit table, the first row of the table is the top army.
- **Unit counts** are the numbers in the page's unit table (icon + count). Pages 122, 123 and 124 have no unit table, so their counts were taken from the map. Map counts were cross-checked against the table for every scenario. Mismatches are listed under each scenario and in section 4.
- **Codes:** LI, LB (light bow), LS (light sling), AX, WA, MI, HI, LC, MC, HC, EL, HCH, LDR. New codes: **LBC** (light bow cavalry), **HWM** (heavy war machine), **CAM** (camel; the site's code is MCM).
- "Not stated" means the page does not say.
- **Book vs web:** the Expansion 1 Contents page says the box has "1 Scenario Book containing 21 battle scenarios". Pages 101–121 were published on 11 May 2008 and 122–124 on 16 June 2008, and only 122–124 lack a unit table. This suggests, but does not prove, that 122–124 were added after the book (as web or official supplements). All 24 are tagged "Expansion 1" and written by "GMT Games".
- **Command cards:** the Expansion 1 contents list no new cards, so the 60 base cards are enough. Hand sizes in these scenarios are 4, 5 or 6.

## 1. Scenarios

### 101 Marathon (490 BC)
- **Top:** Greek Army, Greek blocks. Leader (page): Callimachus. Map leaders: Callimachus, Miltiades. **6 cards. Moves first.**
- **Bottom:** Persian Army, Eastern Kingdom blocks. Leader (page): Datis. Map leaders: Datis, Artaphernes. **5 cards.**
- **Victory:** 6 banners.
- **Special rules:**
  - The stream is fordable.
  - Sea hexes and hills are impassable.
- **Units:**
  - Greek: LI 2, AX 3, MI 2, HI 4, LDR 2.
  - Persian: LI 2, LB 3, AX 6, MI 2, MC 2, LDR 2.
- **Terrain:**
  - Sea/coast along the whole left board edge (beach + sea tiles): impassable.
  - About 10 hill hexes on the right edge, labelled "Hills Impassable".
  - A stream (river) along the Persian rear, all fordable.
- **New needs:**
  - Sea/coast art. The rule itself maps onto the existing impassable `lake`.
  - Impassable hills map onto `steep`.

### 107 Granicus (334 BC)
- **Top:** Macedonian Army, Greek blocks. Leader (page): Alexander. Map leaders: Alexander, Ptolemy, Parmenio. **6 cards. Moves first.**
- **Bottom:** Persian Army, Eastern Kingdom blocks. Leaders: Mithridates, Rhoesaces, Spithridates. **4 cards.**
- **Victory:** 6 banners.
- **Special rules:**
  - **River:** the Granicus River is fordable.
  - **Alexander:** a unit with Alexander attached battles with **+1 die in close combat**.
  - **Companion Cavalry:** a special unit (the MC with Alexander). It ignores 1 sword symbol in close combat and may ignore 1 flag.
  - **Poor Persian Leadership**, for all three satraps:
    - Their leader symbols convert to hits only for the unit the leader is attached to, not for adjacent units.
    - A "Leadership" Command card orders only the unit the leader is attached to, not adjacent units.
    - A satrap still lets the unit he is attached to ignore 1 flag.
- **Units:**
  - Macedonian: LI 2, LB 1, AX 2, MI 1, HI 2, LC 1, MC 2, LDR 3.
  - Persian: LI 2, LB 2, AX 2, MI 2, LC 2, MC 3, HC 1, LDR 3.
  - **Mismatch:** the map shows the two units under Rhoesaces and Spithridates as **"M" (MI, 4 blocks)**. The map therefore has Persian MI 4 and MC 1, while the table says MI 2 and MC 3. Both total 14 units.
- **Terrain:** one straight river across the full width, between the armies. No other terrain.
- **New needs:**
  - Alexander's +1 die.
  - Companion abilities (ignore a sword).
  - Leader effects that apply to the attached unit only, and Leadership cards that order the attached unit only.

### 112 Hellespont (323 BC)
- **Top:** Craterus' Successor Army, **Carthaginian blocks**. Leader (page): Craterus. Map leaders: Craterus, Neoptolemus. **5 cards. Moves first.**
- **Bottom:** Eumenes' Successor Army, Greek blocks. Leader (page): Eumenes. Map leaders: Eumenes, Alcetus. **6 cards.**
- **Victory:** 6 banners.
- **Special rules** (both sides):
  - **Losing a leader costs a card:** if one of your leaders is eliminated, your hand shrinks by one Command card.
    - If the leader is lost during your own turn, you do not draw a new card at the end of that turn.
    - If the leader is lost during the opponent's turn, the opponent picks one of your cards at random and it is discarded.
  - **Instant win:** if you eliminate both opposing leaders, you win at once.
- **Units:**
  - Craterus: LI 1, LB 1, AX 4, MI 3, HI 3, MC 2, LDR 2.
  - Eumenes: LB 2, AX 4, MI 2, HI 2, LC 1, MC 4, LDR 2.
- **Terrain:** all plain.
- **New needs:**
  - A permanent hand-size reduction on leader loss, including a random discard chosen by the opponent.
  - A "kill all enemy leaders" instant win (the existing named-leader sudden death covers only one named leader).

### 116 Heraclea (280 BC)
- **Top:** Epirote Army, Greek blocks. Leader (page): Pyrrhus. Map leaders: Pyrrhus, Leonatus. **5 cards. Moves first.**
- **Bottom:** Roman Army, Roman blocks. Leader (page): "Consular Valerius Laevinus". Map leaders: Laevinus, Cassius. **5 cards.**
- **Victory:** 7 banners.
- **Special rules:**
  - **River Siris:** passable only at the bends in the river. All other river hexes are impassable.
  - **Fright at First Sight:** a Roman infantry unit in close combat with an elephant unit may not ignore any flags rolled by that elephant. This holds even if the Roman unit has an attached leader or is supported by two adjacent friendly units.
- **Units:**
  - Epirote: LI 1, LB 3, AX 2, MI 2, HI 4, LC 1, MC 2, EL 2, LDR 2.
  - Roman: LI 3, AX 1, MI 4, HI 3, MC 4, LDR 2.
- **Terrain:**
  - **River Siris** along the bottom (Roman) board row:
    - 2 straight hexes at the bottom-left, then a bend hex that turns off the board.
    - On the right, a bend hex re-entering the board, then 1 straight hex.
    - A Roman MC starts on each bend hex.
  - 2 hills at the top right.
- **New needs:**
  - Bends-only passability: the existing Crimissos-style per-hex ford flag.
  - Fright at First Sight: no flag-ignoring of any kind for Roman infantry against elephant flags.

## 3. Consolidated engine gaps

### 3.1 New unit types

**Light Bow Cavalry (LBC).** Needed by 109 (Persian ×1), 110 (Scythian ×4), 111 (Macedonian ×2), 113 (×1 each side), 114 (×1 each side), 121 (Seleucid ×1). Source: the Expansion 1 "New Unit Types" page.

| Property | Rule |
|---|---|
| Class | Light (green circle), mounted cavalry. 3 blocks on the maps. |
| Move | 1–4 hexes, and may battle after moving. |
| Ranged combat | Range 3 hexes. 2 dice if it did not move before firing, 1 die if it moved. |
| Close combat | May stay or move 1–4 hexes, then fight an adjacent enemy with 2 dice. No hits on swords. Ignores 1 flag if supported. |
| Evade | May always evade. |
| Momentum | After a successful initial close combat it may advance into the vacated hex, then optionally move 1 more hex. The extra hex is not needed for the bonus combat, and it cannot move the extra hex after a bonus combat. |
| Retreat | 4 hexes per flag, plus 1 extra hex per flag rolled by an elephant or a camel. |

Engine side effects: LBC must be added to "light units" for card targeting, and to the elephant dice table (presumably 2 dice, like LC; not stated).

**Heavy War Machine (HWM).** Needed by 110 (Macedonian ×2) and 118 (Roman ×2); 2 blocks each.

The Expansion 1 unit page does **not** define HWM. It only says light war machines and light chariots are on the reference sheet but unused. The rules below come from the site's HWM summary page and a third-party transcription of the core rulebook (rulespal.com):

| Property | Rule |
|---|---|
| Class | Heavy (red square). Treated as heavy foot for ordering and for taking hits. |
| Move | 1 hex, and it may not battle after moving. It cannot fire if it moved. Excluded from Double Time's 2-hex move. |
| Ranged combat | Range **6**. Dice follow the general rule (2 if it did not move). The site summary gives "Hold 2 / Move 0". |
| Close combat | 2 dice, only while staying in position. No sword hits. Ignores 1 flag if supported. Never makes a momentum advance or bonus close combat. |
| Evade | May (almost) always evade. The attacker rolls normally, and if the war machine survives it is **removed from the board without giving a banner**. Eliminating it does give a banner. |
| Retreat | 1 hex per flag (site summary; the transcription only implies it). |
| Terrain | May not enter broken ground. May not move, evade or retreat into marsh. |

**Camel (CAM / site code MCM).** Needed by 121 (Seleucid ×1, 3 blocks; on the map but not in the page table). Source: Expansion 1 "New Unit Types".

| Property | Rule |
|---|---|
| Class | Medium (blue triangle), mounted. |
| Move | 1–3 hexes, and may battle. |
| Ranged combat | None. |
| Close combat | 3 dice attacking, **2 when battling back**. Sword hits per the site summary. Ignores 1 flag if supported. **May ignore one symbol hit when the enemy attacking or battling back is cavalry or chariot.** |
| Evade | May evade foot units and heavy mounted units. |
| Momentum | Advance plus bonus close combat. |
| Retreat | 3 hexes. |

Camel interactions with other units, from the unit summary pages and the rulebook transcription:
- LC, MC, LBC and HCH retreat 1 extra hex per flag rolled by a camel. The site's HC summary lists only elephants.
- An elephant rolls 3 dice against a camel.

**Special-unit variants of existing types** (these are not new types):
- **Immortals (108):** MI that can fire like a bow unit.
- **Bow-armed AX (123):** AX that fire as "regular Bow Infantry".
- **Companions (107–111):** MC with extra abilities (3.3).
- **Sacred Band (104) and Silver Shields (113, 114):** see 3.3.

### 3.2 New terrain

- **Rampart (hexside-facing):** 102 Himera (about 7 hexes), 118 Beneventum (4 hexes). No scenario page states rules for it; the site's Rampart page (listed under Expansion 6 in its rules index) gives these:
  - No movement effect.
  - It protects the **two forward-facing hexsides** only. The engine needs an orientation per hex.
  - In close combat, a unit attacked across a rampart hexside ignores 1 sword and may ignore 1 flag.
  - Against ranged fire through a forward rampart hexside, it may ignore 1 flag.
  - Mounted units get no benefit.
  - It does not block line of sight.
- **Seacoast / sea:** 101, 102, 108. The site's Seacoast page says it is impassable (no moving, retreating, evading or escaping onto it) and does not block line of sight. Its rules match the existing `lake` exactly: in `src/engine/terrain.ts` the lake is impassable and is not in `terrainBlocksLOS`. Only new tile art is needed: beach-edge coast. 106's gulf already uses the lake-tile style.
- **Impassable hills** (101, 113) can reuse `steep` (impassable, blocks LOS). The difference is that `steep` blocks LOS as a flat block, while real hills use the hill/plateau LOS rule. The pages do not say which LOS behaviour applies to an impassable hill.
- **Fordable river with no dice caps (108 Pinarus):** a variant of the per-hex ford with the 2-dice close-combat and 1-die ranged caps switched off. Whether the "stop on entry" rule still applies is not stated.
- Everything else (hills, impassable hills as `steep`, forest, broken ground, fortified camps in both the palisade and tent styles, rivers that are fordable, impassable, or fordable only at bends) is already supported.

### 3.3 New special-rule mechanisms

1. **Alexander bonus** (107, 108, 109, 110, 111): the unit with Alexander attached rolls +1 close-combat die.
2. **Companion Cavalry** (107, 108, 109, 110, 111 ×2): a special-unit flag giving "ignore 1 sword symbol in close combat" and "may ignore 1 flag". The engine needs a generic special-unit ability set: sword-ignore, flag-ignore, helmet-always-hits, ranged fire.
3. **Sacred Band / Silver Shields on other unit types** (104 Theban MI; 113 and 114 Eumenes HI): the existing `sacredBand` behaviour (helmets always hit, ignore 1 flag) extended to Greek MI/HI. Expansion 1's "New Game Mechanic" page formalises **Special Unit blocks**:
   - The marker does not count as a loss.
   - It moves with the unit and cannot be transferred.
   - The abilities last until the unit's last block is gone.
4. **Immortals** (108): an MI with bow ranged combat.
5. **Bow-armed Auxilia** (123): AX with bow ranged combat.
6. **Poor Persian Leadership** (107): a per-leader flag "attached-only".
   - Helmet hits count only for the attached unit.
   - Leadership cards (Leadership Any Section, Inspired Left/Center/Right Leadership) order only the attached unit.
   - The satrap still gives the attached unit its flag-ignore.
7. **Leader loss shrinks the hand** (112), for both sides:
   - Lost on your own turn: skip the end-of-turn draw.
   - Lost on the opponent's turn: the opponent picks one of your cards at random and it is discarded.
   - The hand size is permanently reduced by 1.
8. **Instant win when all enemy leaders are eliminated** (112). This generalises the existing single named-leader sudden death.
9. **Fright at First Sight** (116): a Roman infantry unit may not ignore flags rolled by an elephant in close combat, with no leader, support or other exceptions. This needs a faction or army tag on units.
10. **Leader placement phase** (117): after the deal, the Roman places 2 leaders, then the Epirote places 2. The AI and UI need a setup decision.
11. **Roman Tactical Flexibility, optional** (120, 121, 124): an unsupported "Greek" HI battling back against a Roman MI or HI rolls 3 dice. This needs per-scenario optional rules or toggles, a way to tell which army counts as "Greek", and the "unsupported" definition.
12. **Camp capture banner for one side** (114). The wording matches base 011 Baecula, so check that the existing mechanism can name the capturing side and the specific camp hex.
13. **Lone leader at setup** (102 Hamilcar; in effect 117 before placement). No base scenario starts with a lone leader, so check that setup supports it.
14. **Not needed:**
    - There are no scythed-chariot rules; Gaugamela's chariots are plain HCH.
    - No hand-size growth rules.
    - No reserves.
    - No exit-the-board banners.
    - No new command cards.

### 3.4 New block colours and armies

- **Greek blocks (light blue)** are used by Greek, Theban, Macedonian, Epirote, Syracusan (102, 122), the Successors (Eumenes 112–114, Antigonus 115) and the Seleucids (119, 123; and 121 per the map).
  - Unit art needed: LI, LS, LB, AX, MI, WA, HI, LC, LBC, MC, HC, EL, HCH, HWM, Camel, leader.
  - **The Syracusans use Greek blocks in Expansion 1**, whereas the engine's `syracuse` faction draws them with Roman blocks.
- **Eastern Kingdom blocks (light tan)** are used by Persian, Spartan (104, 105), Phocian, Scythian, Indian, Antigonus (113, 114), Seleucus (115) and Ptolemaic armies; also by 121's Seleucids per the War Council text.
  - Unit art needed: LI, LS, LB, AX, MI, WA, HI, LC, LBC, MC, HC, EL, HCH, leader.
- **Carthaginian blocks** for a non-Carthaginian army: Craterus (112). This is data only.
- **Roman unit types missing from the base Roman art** (base scenarios use only AX, HI, LB, LC, LI, LS, MC, MI, WA for Rome):
  - EL: 120, 121, 124.
  - HC: 124.
  - HWM: 118.
- **Ipsus (115):** the 4th Seleucid elephant uses a core-game block (cosmetic).
- The engine's `Faction` type (`rome | carthage | syracuse`) needs at least `greek` and `eastern`. Army names should be free text, because many armies share one block set.

## 4. Ambiguities and things not determined

1. **102 Himera:**
   - The page has no Special Rules section at all.
   - The map shows 3 fortified camps and about 7 rampart hexes, but there is no camp-banner rule and no rampart rule.
   - Its seacoast is not stated to be impassable (the site's terrain definition says it is).
   - The map image is a lower-resolution redraw (`102-Himera.jpg`) with no move-first star.
   - It is not clear whether the printed book had rules here.
2. **118 Beneventum:** no special rules on the page, though the map has 3 camps and 4 ramparts. The War Council leader text ("Consuls Decius and Sulpicius") looks copied from 117; the map names Dentatus.
3. **Rampart rules** exist on the site only as a general terrain page (indexed under Expansion 6). Expansion 1 does not define them, and the rampart facing on these two maps has to be read from the art.
4. **107 Granicus:** the unit table says Persian MI 2 / MC 3, but the map shows MI 4 / MC 1 (the two satrap-led central units are drawn as "M" with 4 blocks). Which one is right was not determined.
5. **121 Magnesia:**
   - The War Council says the Seleucids use "Persian" (Eastern Kingdom) blocks, but the table icons and the map are Greek blue.
   - The camel unit on the map is missing from the unit table.
   - With Eastern blocks, it is unclear whether "Greek heavy infantry" in Tactical Flexibility still means the Seleucid HI.
6. **117 Asculum:**
   - Victory is 6 banners per the War Council but 7 per the page tag.
   - The leader-placement rule gives no limits (on own units only? any hex? one per unit?). Not stated.
7. **122, 123, 124:** no unit tables, so all counts come from the map (zoomed and cross-checked, but still a reading). Their War Council text has no "Use … blocks" line; blocks were inferred from the CSS class and the map colour.
8. **Companions "ignore 1 sword symbol in Close Combat":** the page does not say whether this means swords rolled against the Companions (most likely) or something else.
9. **Alexander +1 die:** not stated whether it applies when battling back, or how it interacts with terrain dice caps (cards add after caps per the terrain pages; Alexander is not mentioned).
10. **Immortals (108):** "armed with bows and follow Ranged Combat rules". Range and dice are not stated (bow infantry is range 3, 2 dice still / 1 moved). Also not stated whether the MI may move then fire.
11. **Bow-armed AX (123):** "as for regular Bow Infantry". Presumably range 3 like LB. Not stated whether the AX "no fire after moving 2 hexes" limit still applies, or whether other AX rules are unchanged.
12. **Silver Shields (113, 114):** the wording lacks Leuctra's "even when a leader is not attached or adjacent". Presumably the same rule, but that is not stated.
13. **Issus:**
    - "Fordable with no battle dice reductions": not stated whether units must still stop on entering.
    - The "Amanus Mtns." hills and the "Mediterranean Sea" have no rules on the page; they are read here as normal hills and impassable seacoast.
14. **Heraclea:** "only passable at the bends". Not stated whether the bend hexes follow fordable-river rules (stop, 2-dice cap) or are clear terrain. The base Crimissos precedent says "fordable".
15. **Roman Tactical Flexibility:** "unsupported" is not defined. The base definition is presumably adjacency to 2 friendly units.
16. **Fright at First Sight:** "Roman infantry unit" presumably means all Roman foot (LI, LB, LS, AX, MI, HI). Not stated.
17. **Camel:** "ignore one symbol hit". Which symbol is not stated (the site summary just says "1 symbol hit"). Not determined whether HC retreats extra against camels.
18. **HWM:** Expansion 1 has no HWM rules. Range 6, the dice and the evade-removal rule come from the site summary and a third-party rulebook transcription, not from official Expansion 1 text. HWM retreat distance is from the site summary only.
19. **Book vs web:** whether 122–124 are in the printed scenario book (which has 21 scenarios) was not confirmed.
20. Hex counts for terrain ("about N") are approximate readings of the map images. Exact hex coordinates were not extracted.
